## Chapter 10 — The Greenhouse

Mark drives Jack across a waking settlement, sending residents back indoors and briefing
Doctor Mercer by radio. At the greenhouse, Aiden is alone with a pesticide fogger; Jack
talks him down. The spider in the grass past the pavement is a jumping spider (Aiden
knows them as the aphid-eaters he never sprays), about the size of a small car rather
than a delivery truck, cold and waiting for the sun. Sarah sees why it is there: the grow
lights ran all night and the glass is covered in moths and flies. Ends at seven o'clock,
as the roof vents open on their timer and the spider's eyes tilt toward the sound.

## Chapter 11 — Slow Is Invisible

Aiden's override closes most vents; three on the west feeder Lena isolated last night
stay open. Jack climbs to the catwalk and cranks them shut by hand, slowly ("Slow is
invisible. Fast is food."). The warmed spider jumps onto the roof for a fly, cracks a
pane, looks Jack in the face from a meter away, takes the fly and leaves. Jack cuts his
hand. Sarah links the jump to last night's no-trail marks; Aiden points out jumpers do
not hunt in the dark, so the marks stay open. Sarah speaks the settlement's first
creature rules. Ends on Unit Four: a crowd at the community center asking for Jack by
name.

## Chapter 12 — One Clear Message

Aiden comes with them; Sarah insists on joining. Doctor Mercer meets them in person and
orders both Bennetts to medical afterward. Lena routes the hall microphone to every
speaker in the settlement. Jack tells everyone the truth about the activation and the
scale, gives the instructions, and Lena plays the greenhouse footage. Questions: how
small, can it be reversed (he does not know and will not try until he understands it),
outside help (silent), a daughter in Boston. Ends on Paul Harlan of the machine shop:
machines don't switch themselves on, so who did? Jack has not decided whether to tell
the settlement the activation was deliberate.
