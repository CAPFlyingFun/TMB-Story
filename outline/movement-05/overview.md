## Chapter 14 — Who Was It

Jack answers Paul Harlan over every speaker: somebody activated TOMBS on purpose, with
Jack's credentials, and he does not know who. He says openly that he is keeping the rest off
the public address because the person responsible may be listening. Security, not the
laboratory, will investigate; Paul will see what can be shown without tipping them off.
Food is being counted. A barefoot young man who won't believe it is shown the live camera at
the end of the western road. Jack's answer to "what do we actually do?": today, we count.
The address ends, and Lena calls privately: the moment Jack said "restricted", the whole town
started filling bathtubs.

## Chapter 15 — Bathtubs

Lena drops the pressure to the housing blocks; medical, the kitchens and the utility station
keep full pressure. Security fields the complaints. Doctor Mercer marches Jack to medical:
four stitches. Sarah's scan: the baby's heartbeat is strong and he kicks; her blood pressure
is a little high, so rest and a recheck tonight. Sarah talks her way into resting in a chair
at the utility station. Paul Harlan is already there, arguing about his shop's water.

## Chapter 16 — The Water Clock

Paul's coolant goes on the list, and his night shift had no time to fill a bathtub. At the
gauges: two tanks, about five hundred thousand liters full, refilled every night from the
reservoir between midnight and five, so they were near their low when the intake was cut at
eleven. About three hundred and fifty thousand liters are left; normal use is about a hundred
thousand a day. The limits: twenty liters a person a day in two supply windows, toilets
flushed with used washing water, medical and kitchens on their own allowance, the greenhouse
on treated water from the wastewater plant (Paul's fittings and night shift), a drinking
point at the community center, and stored water stays with whoever stored it. Fifteen
thousand liters a day: a little over three weeks, if nothing leaks, breaks or cheats. Lena
announces it at noon. Sarah looks at the grass: they are only counting the water inside.

## Chapter 17 — Dew

The dawn footage: every blade at the edge hung with water from about half past six, gone by
nine. Aiden explains dew and guttation, and that insects drown in the drops. Jack estimates
more than two hundred liters in one drop, ten people's water for a day. Mark holds the line,
then agrees to a plan: a small roped team, radio every two minutes, the very end of the
western road, one jug and a sample, back by nine, at first light the day after tomorrow. Aiden
wants to come. Mercer's evening check: Sarah's pressure is better but still high. Jack and
Sarah home for the first time since the activation. The baby kicks for Jack's promise. Far
out, something heavy lands in the grass.

## Next chapter start

Jack and Sarah's living room, late on the night of March sixth, outside lights off, Sarah
asleep against him, Jack awake after a distant impact. The next day, March seventh, is for
planning the expedition (B4): roles, ropes, radios, how to draw water from a drop without
touching it, who goes. The team goes at first light on March eighth, to the end of the
western road and no farther, back by nine. Mercer rechecks Sarah's blood pressure on the
evening of March seventh.
