## Chapter 24 — First Gray

A quarter past four. Sarah is already awake with the feeds ("He's been kicking since three") and
tapes the glove and bag on Jack's hand. Paul drives the trough himself and is allowed as far as the
cart. Aiden at the greenhouse door: five degrees, every star. The trough comes off the flatbed on
planks (one loud scrape, nothing stirs) and is carried, never dragged, to the crack across the road
that Sarah measured from the March 9 footage. Mark's standing call: if the drop over the crack turns
out to be under the retreat, they move. First gray: it is. Sarah finds a second leaf two meters left,
about half the water, nothing above it. Mark chooses it; four people walk the trough over in the gray.
Aiden: four degrees, not a cloud. Six thirty-four. "Go." Jack slides the fork under the leaf.

## Chapter 25 — One Shake

The leaf is heavier than anything in the drill; sprung back, it throws its drop forward, straight
at the people's side, and the board takes it. A shower of edge beads follows; one soaks Jack's
shoulder; Paul's collar keeps the glove dry. The stems sway; at "Stop" they freeze; a leg tip shows
at the retreat opening (four degrees) and goes back in. A gnat that came down with the shower kicks
in the trough; the lids go on slow and it goes quiet. Six thirty-eight: Mark won't risk carrying it
full ("I'd rather loud than wet"); the trough is hauled back on Unit Four's spare line, leaking a
little at the corners. Sun on the tops at six forty-two with the team off the edge and halfway back;
the spider comes out and basks. Paul sees it ("It's smaller than I thought. No. It isn't."). At the
station the spigot fills five twenty-liter jugs and half a sixth: **a hundred and ten liters**.
Aiden, from the feed: the leg came out two seconds after the gnat started kicking, not when the stem
moved. "Every leaf we shake is going to shake one down."

## Chapter 26 — Wet Asphalt

The gnat, drowned, goes into Aiden's jar ("Everything out there is eating something. We just don't
know the list yet"). The water goes to the community center kitchens to be boiled in the big pots.
Mark's board: what worked (board, collar, lid, strips, rope; Unit Four praised for tying off twice)
and what didn't (wrong place in the dark, two minutes lost; leaking corners). Lena's tank reading:
about twenty-five thousand liters on March 10; use is closer to fifteen hundred a day;
about sixteen days. A hundred and ten liters is not quite two hours of the town's water.
Mark: one leaf near that retreat on any morning, never the one under it; "find me somewhere else to
shake." Sarah: there are cameras at the end of every road, mostly unwatched since March 6; she will
list every leaf tip over asphalt, its drops, and any silk. Paul: corners sealed, a second, narrower
trough. Mercer: pressure holding at "a little better"; the hand dry ("Somebody finally kept a bag
dry"); the crib has its second rail. Night: on the recording nobody watched live, at seven oh four the
spider came onto the asphalt, drank from the shower's puddles, and followed the trough's wet line
halfway toward the cart, stopping where the road was dry, then went back.

## Next chapter start

The night of March tenth, Jack and Sarah on the couch with the seven oh four recording just ended.
Nobody else has seen it yet. Mark has not agreed to a second shake; Paul is building a second trough;
Sarah has begun the survey of the other road ends from the couch. The next clear, cold morning is
unknown. Whatever happens next at the western road has to reckon with the wet line.
