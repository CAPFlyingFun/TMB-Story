## Chapter 18 — Nothing Bare Touches Water

Jack watched the grass all night. Mark: last night's impact was logged by the perimeter
sensors at eleven forty, too far out for any camera; it goes with the others. At nine, at the
utility station: Paul's greenhouse line went in at three, under tarps; the kitchens' count is
about six weeks of food at careful portions. Mark's whiteboard of what can go wrong (spider,
drop, ground, birds, radio, "brave"). Aiden: never a cup, water climbs; wick it. Paul's
four-meter pole with a sponge, a funnel, a twenty-liter jug. Team: Mark on the rope, Jack on
the pole, Aiden for the sample, Unit Four at the cart. Toes on the asphalt. Sarah, on the
greenhouse cameras from home, finds the jumping spider's silk retreat above the end of the
road: it comes out at first sun (six forty-two) and hunts from just before seven. Half an
hour. Mark writes "Be boring."

## Chapter 19 — No Lights

Lena reads the food count at noon. Drills in the yard behind the station; the word "stop".
Unit Four learns the knots. Mercer's evening check: blood pressure the same as high, not
worse; no protein. She gloves and bags Jack's stitched hand. Sarah makes Jack repeat the rules;
he sleeps. Before dawn on March eighth Mark drives dark to the end of the western road, the
rope is tied to the cart's tow hitch, radio every two minutes with Lena, Sarah listening from
the couch. A robin's song out on the island comes through the radio. In the gray the drops
appear; the biggest hangs three meters up over the last meter of asphalt, under the retreat.
Jack lifts the pole.

## Chapter 20 — Twelve Liters

The sponge soaks a drop without bursting it; water creeps down the pole toward Jack's hands.
Aiden takes a dew sample from a small edge drop. A midge stuck in a low drop overnight warms
and struggles. The spider comes out at first sun; "Stop." They freeze. It jumps for the midge;
the blade bends, the big drop falls and lands like a wave, knocking Aiden flat and dragging him
toward the soil; the rope hauls him back. The spider looks at him, but it already has food, and
climbs back to its retreat. Back to the cart: twelve liters, a sealed sample, Aiden soaked and
chilled, Jack's stitches wet (Sarah has already called Mercer). At the station: the dew is
low in minerals, clean enough to filter and boil, further tests pending. Twelve liters against
fifteen thousand a day. Jack's idea from the fall: shake the drops down onto a trough at the
road end. Paul: a gutter, a day, and a drip collar for the pole. Back at seven fifty, before
nine. Aiden: it didn't come for us because we were boring. Mark: shaking grass is fast, and
fast is food. He hasn't said yes.
