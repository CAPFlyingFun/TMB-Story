# Mini-Arc 1 — REVISED PROPOSAL (Chapters 1–10) — "First Field Assignment"

**Status: PROPOSED, 2026-09-16, awaiting Joshua. On approval this file replaces
`mini-arc-01.md` and the current Chapter 1 draft is retired.**

**Purpose:** two newly independent eighteen-year-olds on their first real field
work, having a wonderful time until the sea takes it back; the ordinary world of
the Bennett kitchen; then a mundane crash and an impossible moment inside it.
Mini-Arc 1 now carries the crash and the first fit of the two visions, which the
old version held back to Chapters 7–10.
**Outside clock:** first week of October to the last week of October, Year 0.
Caleb and Nora are 18, a few months past their birthday, three weeks into
three-month seasonal contracts as field assistants at Alder Sound Field Station.
**Answered:** nothing formally, by design; but by Chapter 10 the twins hold one
detail neither could have invented.
**Opened:** was the vision real (M04); why did it split (M05); what is Mom not
saying (M13, now with "Where was he?" on the page); who has been keeping Dad's
old equipment alive (proposed M34); is the family's story true, and who wrote it
(proposed M33).
**Emotional arc:** joy → ordinary warmth → fear → the two of them alone with a
thing that fits together → a decision to look.
**Hook out:** in a weatherproof box their father built on a tidal island, a card
in his handwriting, in the notation Nora saw scratched into a wall in the dark.

## Setting and people (all provisional, swap freely)

- **Alder Sound Field Station** — a small, mostly honest coastal research
  nonprofit on the sound north of Port Alder: a dozen staff, an old boathouse,
  a lab with a leaking skylight, seasonal assistants every fall. NOT the outside
  organization. Tidewater Instruments (Rachel's employer) services its loggers,
  which is how the twins heard about the openings. They qualified on their own:
  Caleb on a summer of trail-crew and a reference from a biology teacher, Nora
  on a spreadsheet nobody asked for.
- **Tern Island** — a wooded bird reserve in the sound, reachable on foot across
  a gravel bar for about three hours either side of low water, and by skiff
  otherwise. Old monitoring gear on its summit predates most of the staff.
- **Dr. Ines Marchetti** — field program lead, mid-40s, dry, unhurried, takes
  the kids seriously without making a speech about it.
- **Marco Sato** — technician, 24, drives the station van, teases everyone,
  will carry the guilt of the crash for a while.
- **Gus Pelletier** — caretaker and boat operator, 60s, thirty years at the
  station, remembers "a Mercer" who put half the old gear in himself.
- **The enclosure** — a weatherproof monitoring box on Tern Island's summit,
  aluminium, older than the twins, a stamped tag reading `D.M.` and a year.

Retired from the previous version: Harlow's Boatyard, the drowned engine, the
customer, the Mayor, Nora's library digitization job. Kept: Port Alder, the
Bennett home, Tidewater Instruments, Mercy General.

### Chapter 0001 — The Bar
- **POV:** Caleb · **Target:** 2,300–2,500 (the set piece earns it)
- **Opening situation:** 6 a.m. at the station boathouse, low tide in two
  hours. Marco loading the van with the enthusiasm of a man who is not
  carrying any of it. Caleb and Nora arguing about whose pack is heavier.
  Nora has brought a luggage scale. Of course she has.
- **Scenes / beats:**
  1. The crossing on foot: the gravel bar shining, the island getting bigger,
     Ines's briefing (transect, bird count, pollinator sweep, swap the
     logger's battery and download it, back by one). Caleb in mud to the
     knee within ten minutes; a gull steals half his sandwich; Nora
     photographs both for the record.
  2. The work, and the fun of it: the island at low water is enormous and
     alive. Caleb on the pollinator sweep, off-transect within twenty
     minutes because an ant colony is moving its brood uphill in the middle
     of the day, a river of pale grubs carried in jaws. "The transect is that
     way." "I know." "You're walking the other way." "Also know that."
     Nora at the logger, downloading, cross-checking the station's printed
     tide table against the logger's own pressure trace because that is the
     kind of thing she does for fun.
  3. Two alarms from two instruments: Caleb notices the far spit has gone
     quiet, the shorebirds up and gone, the crabs off the mud; the ants are
     still climbing. Nora notices the barometer has dropped like a stone in
     the last hour and the wind has backed; her own arithmetic says the
     water will be over the bar's low point well before the printed table
     says. They find each other. "Your ants and my numbers agree."
  4. They bring it to Ines. She doesn't wave them off and she doesn't panic:
     she reads the logger herself, looks at the sky and the spit, radios the
     station, and calls the retreat ninety minutes early. The adults were
     not fools; the surge is a real, unforecast thing, and two new kids
     caught it from two directions.
  5. The pack-out: the twins are useful, not in charge. Caleb ferries gear
     and finds the firm line across the bar as the first sheet of water
     slides over the low point; Nora keeps the count sheets dry, calls the
     depth at the low point, keeps the clock. Marco carrying two packs and
     narrating. Everyone across with water at the shins, laughing the way
     you laugh when you were scared thirty seconds ago.
  6. Ines, dry as the mainland: "Good catch. Both of you." Caleb: "It was the
     ants." Nora: "It was the barometer." Ines: "It was both, which is why
     I'm keeping you."
- **Character:** the split shown, never explained: he reads living things,
  she reads numbers, both were right, neither alone was enough. Youth: mud,
  the gull, the packs, showing off for Marco. Rachel is off-page except as
  the person Nora texts a photo of Caleb in the mud.
- **Creature/world:** the ant brood-move, the crab retreat, the birds'
  silence, a glimpse (only a glimpse) of the old aluminium enclosure on the
  summit as they hurry past it; Caleb notices it is the one dry, cared-for
  thing on a neglected hilltop and thinks nothing of it.
- **Mysteries:** none formally. The enclosure is a picture, not a clue yet.
- **Continuity:** no injuries; wet to the knees; the count unfinished, the
  logger's spare battery not swapped — a return trip is needed (sets up Ch. 3).
  Ines, Marco, Gus (radio voice only), the van, the station all established.
- **Ending type:** funny character beat — in the van, the luggage scale:
  Nora's pack was heavier. By four hundred grams. "I said." "You said."
- **Hands off:** home.

### Chapter 0002 — Sixty-Two Grams of Salt
- **POV:** Nora · **Target:** 2,100–2,300
- **Opening situation:** the van drops them at the bottom of the hill at
  dusk, filthy and delighted. In the front hall, before they have their
  boots off, Nora sees Rachel fold a letter in three and slide it into her
  back pocket. Caleb, behind her, sees nothing but dinner.
- **Scenes / beats:**
  1. The kitchen. Rachel in the Tidewater fleece, straight from the bench.
     The twins tell the day in stereo: Caleb's version has ants and a gull
     and a heroic sandwich; Nora's version has a pressure trace and a
     timetable. Same day, two reports. Rachel, who services the very logger
     Nora downloaded, asks one professional question too many about the
     surge and then makes them eat.
  2. The two-count, in the car earlier, deciding which version to tell Mom
     (the fun one), and again at the table over the last of the bread.
     "We built it the hard way" gets its first airing when Rachel says the
     station manager told her the two of them "have that twin thing."
  3. The photograph on the shelf by the door, the man on a dock in a green
     jacket, squinting; eyes slide past it. Then Caleb, out of nowhere,
     because the station's hallway has photos from the nineties: "Did Dad
     ever work out there?" Rachel: "Your father did research." And the fixed
     words, said as a door closing: research trip, the boat lost off the
     coast, nobody ever found, eighteen years ago. "That's the whole of it.
     Pass your brother the salt."
  4. Rachel's worry about the job, read by the twins as the obvious thing:
     of course Mom doesn't love them working somewhere Dad might have been.
  5. Nora, later, on the stairs with a glass of water, sees Rachel standing
     at the shelf. Looking at the photograph. The one nobody looks at.
- **Character:** Nora's protectiveness reading as coldness to Caleb; Rachel's
  warmth and her script in the same breath; Caleb not seeing, Nora seeing.
- **Creature/world:** the ant story retold makes Rachel laugh; a moth at the
  kitchen window; the house at night.
- **Mysteries:** M13 (subtext: the letter; the photograph). Proposed +M33 (is
  the family's story true, and who wrote it) rides on the recitation.
- **Continuity:** letter into back pocket, unread by anyone; the fixed words
  established verbatim; Rachel knows Alder Sound's gear professionally.
- **Ending type:** quiet unsettling line — "Mom was looking at it."
- **Hands off:** the return trip, four days later.

### Chapter 0003 — The Enclosure
- **POV:** Caleb · **Target:** 2,300–2,500
- **Opening situation:** Saturday, the tide wrong for the bar, so Gus runs
  the four of them out in the station skiff. A calm, gold October morning.
  The island at rest is even better than the island in a hurry.
- **Scenes / beats:**
  1. Finishing the work properly: the count completed, the battery swapped,
     Nora's logger download clean. Caleb finds a dew-strung orb-weaver web
     across the trail and refuses to walk through it; the whole team goes
     around. Marco's commentary.
  2. The summit and the enclosure. Gus, waiting by the skiff, wanders up
     with them: "That's from the old programme. Before most of us." Aluminium
     box, weatherproof, a stamped tag: `D.M.` and a year older than the twins.
     Gus: "Mercer. Your name's Bennett, though." "Our mother's." "Huh."
     That's all he says. The twins barely react; Nora reads the year twice.
     Caleb notices the box is dry inside its seals and the padlock is not
     rusted, on a hilltop where everything else is.
  3. Weather turns on the run back: rain, wind, the sound going grey. The
     van on the coast road, Marco driving, Ines following in her own car.
     The twins in the back seat arguing, warm and stupid and tired: "The
     ants were first." "The barometer was right." "The ants were right and
     first."
  4. The crash, mundane and mechanical: on a curve in the rain, an oncoming
     pickup towing a boat trailer, the trailer swaying wide across the
     centre line. Marco brakes and steers right; the van's wheels leave the
     wet road; the guardrail, then the ditch, then a tree. Loud, short,
     exact. No one is at fault in a way that matters.
  5. The impossible thing, inside the impact, from Caleb's side: dark, then a
     man's hoarse voice shouting to other people, "Get them under. Hold the
     line." A sound like rain on a drum, or a hive. Hands on a cable. Wet
     soil. A man's back, turning. Caleb knows it is his father the way you
     know a thing in a dream, and has no reason to. Then rain on the van
     roof, and Marco saying his name.
- **Character:** Caleb having a perfect day and losing it; his instinct to
  act (he is out of his seatbelt reaching for Nora before he knows his wrist
  is broken). Gus's economy. Marco's fear.
- **Creature/world:** the orb-weaver, the island in calm, the enclosure.
- **Mysteries:** M04, M05 begin on the page (introduced formally Ch. 5).
  Daniel's connection to the station is now a fact the audience holds and the
  twins do not yet weigh.
- **Continuity:** Caleb fractured left wrist and cuts; Nora unconscious,
  concussion, cracked rib (established next chapter); Marco bruised,
  whiplash; Ines uninjured, first on scene. Van totalled. The tag's year
  recorded. The count sheets survive in Nora's pack.
- **Ending type:** revelation — the voice, the hands, the cable, and the
  certainty.
- **Hands off:** Mercy General.

### Chapter 0004 — Where Was He
- **POV:** Caleb · **Target:** 2,100–2,300
- **Opening situation:** the ER at Mercy General, Port Alder, in pieces:
  lights, a CT scan, someone counting backward. His wrist set under sedation.
  Then a room, dim, and his mother in the chair.
- **Scenes / beats:**
  1. Surfacing, medicated, frightened, eighteen. "Mom. I saw Dad." Rachel does
     not say "What do you mean." She says, "Where was he?" He tries: under
     something, in the dark, there was a line, people were shouting. She
     asks one more thing, too specific ("Was it raining?"), then: water, the
     blanket, "Sleep." He sleeps. He does not register any of it. The
     audience does.
  2. Morning. Nora in the next bay, awake, grey, furious about the light,
     told to rest and not think. The neurologist, kind and complete: both had
     brief loss of consciousness; both stay one night for observation;
     home tomorrow with concussion instructions. Nora says nothing about
     anything she saw. She is assembling it.
  3. Marco in a neck brace in the doorway, guilty, dramatic, forgiven
     immediately by Caleb and slightly less immediately by Nora. Ines with a
     bag of the twins' things from the van, dry, kind, and the count sheets.
  4. Caleb, watching Rachel hold Nora's hand while Nora sleeps, sees his
     mother clearly for the first time in the story: what eighteen years
     cost, in the way she holds a chart she cannot read.
- **Character:** Caleb's blind spot breaks a little, in the hospital as Rev 1
  intended. Rachel composed for everyone but the audience.
- **Creature/world:** none; a hospital.
- **Mysteries:** M13 (advanced, on the page: "Where was he?"). M04, M05 held.
- **Continuity:** injuries confirmed; one night's observation; discharge next
  day; concussion protocol (no screens, no driving, rest); cast six weeks then
  a brace. Rachel drove in from Tidewater; Marco's truck of guilt begins.
- **Ending type:** emotional beat — Rachel's hand on Nora's, and Caleb seeing.
- **Hands off:** home, and what Nora remembers.

### Chapter 0005 — Halves
- **POV:** Nora · **Target:** 2,100–2,300
- **Opening situation:** discharge; the drive home with Rachel driving very
  carefully; the house with the curtains drawn for Nora's eyes; day one of
  being told not to think, by people who have met her.
- **Scenes / beats:**
  1. Recovery as comedy: Caleb one-handed and bored, Nora banned from screens
     and furious, Rachel enforcing both with the calm of a woman who has
     read the pamphlet. The two-count over something dumb (microwaving a
     thing that should not be microwaved).
  2. Nora has said nothing because she has been building it: the vault of
     roots, the lattice of small lights (she counted: she still has the
     count), a wall scored with marks like nested brackets, a doorway that is
     not a door. She tells Caleb, on the couch, in the afternoon, expecting
     him to tell her it was the concussion.
  3. Caleb goes white. "Who told you about the line?" "What line?" "I said
     there was a line. To Mom." "You didn't say anything to me." Neither
     told the other. His voice fits inside her room; his "get them under"
     fits her doorway; the rhythm of his hive-sound matches her lights. Same
     event. Different attention. It is very hard now to call it suggestion.
  4. Then Caleb, remembering: "She asked me where he was." Nora: "She asked
     what?" "Not what I meant. Where he was." Nora files it in the place she
     keeps the folded letter and the look at the photograph. Three things.
  5. They agree not to tell Mom more. Both know it is a bad sign about the
     family. They do it anyway.
- **Character:** the first vulnerable conversation in years, forced by the
  thing itself. Nora's method (assemble, then speak) paying off; Caleb's
  (speak, then think) having already paid off with Rachel by accident.
- **Creature/world:** the house; rain; nothing alive but them.
- **Mysteries:** +M04, +M05 formally introduced. M13 (advanced: the
  question, the letter, the photograph now one pattern to Nora).
- **Continuity:** the two fragments recorded verbatim (these become the
  canonical vision text); Rachel's question on the record; the decision not
  to tell her.
- **Ending type:** discovery.
- **Hands off:** the week of getting better.

### Chapter 0006 — Painkillers at Two in the Morning
- **POV:** Caleb · **Target:** 2,000–2,200
- **Opening situation:** the recovery week. Headaches, a cast that itches,
  the twins driving each other up the wall in a small house, Rachel coming
  home early every day and pretending she isn't.
- **Scenes / beats:**
  1. The follow-up: the neurologist explains shared post-traumatic
     confabulation, gently and well. Rachel pushes back harder than either
     twin expects and asks one strangely specific question ("Did they both
     report a sound?"), then covers. Nora, who is present this time, adds it
     to the list. Four things.
  2. Caleb wants to ask Rachel straight out. Nora wants to write everything
     down separately, first, and check what can be checked. The first real
     disagreement about how far to take this, loud, in whispers, because Mom
     is downstairs.
  3. Two in the morning. Caleb comes down for a painkiller and finds Rachel
     at the kitchen table with a folder open in front of her, which she
     closes before he is all the way in the room. "Can't sleep?" "Wrist."
     She makes him toast. He is foggy, but this time he registers it, and
     does not say so.
  4. The decision: they go back to work when they're cleared, they look at
     what the station has on that enclosure, and they don't ask Mom. Caleb
     agrees. Barely.
- **Character:** Caleb learning to wait; Nora learning that waiting has a
  cost too; Rachel's composure with a crack in it only the audience can see.
- **Creature/world:** a spider in the bathroom Caleb refuses to kill
  one-handed; the house at night.
- **Mysteries:** M13 (advanced: the folder; the neurologist question).
- **Continuity:** cleared for light duty; Caleb in cast, Nora's headaches
  easing; the folder's existence known to Caleb only.
- **Ending type:** decision.
- **Hands off:** back to the station.

### Chapter 0007 — Light Duty
- **POV:** Nora · **Target:** 2,100–2,300
- **Opening situation:** Nora on desk duty at Alder Sound because she is not
  allowed in the field yet: reconciling the equipment inventory against the
  service records ahead of a grant renewal, which is the most boring job in
  the building and the one she was born for.
- **Scenes / beats:**
  1. The station without the field: the leaking skylight, the coffee, Marco
     in his brace being dramatic, Ines's dry care. Caleb on the dock with
     Gus, one-handed, learning knots and being told he's doing them wrong.
  2. Ordinary work, ordinary discrepancy: the Tern Island enclosure is in
     the inventory as decommissioned in a year that matches the tag; but a
     Tidewater service ticket shows its battery swapped eight years later,
     and again four years after that, under a programme code nobody at the
     station recognizes. The installer's name on the original record is
     blank. She notes it, because she notes everything, and moves on.
  3. Gus, at lunch, when Nora asks who the Mercer was: quiet guy, weekends,
     put half the old gear in himself, then gone. "Gone how?" "Boat." Nora
     hears the fixed words come out of someone else's mouth and does not
     like how that feels.
  4. Rachel's initials on three of the Tidewater service tickets. Her
     mother has been out there.
- **Character:** Nora at her best and most alone; Caleb finding his feet with
  Gus; the station as a place they like, which matters for later.
- **Creature/world:** the station's resident heron; the dock at low water.
- **Mysteries:** proposed +M34 (who has been keeping the enclosure alive).
  M13 (advanced, quietly: Rachel's initials). M30 seed (the programme code).
- **Continuity:** the two service dates; the programme code; Rachel's initials
  on tickets; Gus knew Daniel.
- **Ending type:** quiet unsettling line — Gus: "Funny thing. Somebody's still
  changing the batteries out there."
- **Hands off:** the garage.

### Chapter 0008 — Boxes
- **POV:** Caleb · **Target:** 2,100–2,300

- **Scenes / beats:**
  1. Caleb wants to read everything; Nora, arriving, wants to catalogue it.

  2. Rachel home early, again. She takes the box back a little too fast. The

  3. After: the twins on the roof. Caleb needs a father who was worth

     Rev 1 §3's disagreement, out loud, early.
- **Character:** Caleb's hunger, shown; Rachel's script, heard; the twins
  taking positions on Dad for the first time.
- **Creature/world:** silverfish, the egg sac, moths at the roof light.
- **Mysteries:** M02 (first geographic seed: the tide table). M13 (advanced).
  Proposed M33 (advanced: the words have an order).
- **Continuity:** the tide table's region photographed by Nora; the pocketed

- **Ending type:** piece of evidence.

### Chapter 0009 — The Wall Map
- **POV:** Nora · **Target:** 2,100–2,300
- **Opening situation:** Nora, cleared for screens, starts the wall map that
  will follow her for the rest of Part One: the tide-table region, the
  enclosure's two service dates, the programme code, the year on the tag.
- **Scenes / beats:**
  1. Ordinary work supplies the next fact: the station's old annual reports
     on the break-room shelf, public documents, list programme partners by
     year. The enclosure's programme was run "in partnership with" a lab at
     Cascade State University, in the years before the tag's date. A name
     for a place, not a person. Nora writes it on the map.
  2. Caleb and Nora argue method again and this time land it: he wants to go
     look at the box itself; she wants the records first. They do both. The
     plan: the next low-tide window is Thursday at dusk, off the clock, and
     Nora has the enclosure's lock code from the inventory, which is not
     the same as permission. Two-count.
  3. Rachel, seeing the wall map for the first time, says nothing at all,
     which is louder than anything she could have said.
- **Character:** Nora's obsession beginning, with the wall; Caleb agreeing to
  do it her way and then his; Rachel's silence.
- **Creature/world:** the skirting-board ants in the station break room Nora
  has been timing for weeks.
- **Mysteries:** M30 seed (the university lab, unnamed). M34 (advanced).
  M13 (Rachel's silence).
- **Continuity:** the map exists; the lock code known; Thursday's tide window
  fixed; the twins have not asked Rachel anything since Ch. 8.
- **Ending type:** unanswered question — "Who's changing the batteries?"
- **Hands off:** Thursday.

### Chapter 0010 — Low Water
- **POV:** Caleb · **Target:** 2,300–2,500
- **Opening situation:** Thursday, an hour before sunset, the gravel bar
  shining, two eighteen-year-olds who were told not to do exactly this,
  doing it. Caleb's cast in a bread bag. Nora with a headlamp and a
  timetable.
- **Scenes / beats:**
  1. The crossing, alone this time: the island at dusk is theirs, and it is
     beautiful, and they are a little scared, and it's the best thing that
     has happened since the sandbar. The ants on the summit have finished
     moving house.
  2. The enclosure. The code works. Inside: dry, tidy, a battery newer than
     anything in the inventory, a logger that is still running. And a
     laminated card, old, with hand calculations on it: nested brackets, a
     doubled dot on the inner subscript, the boundary term first.
  3. Nora goes still. Caleb: "What." Nora: "I've seen this." "Where?" "On a
     wall. In the dark. I told you about the wall." The marks she saw in the
     crash are on a card in a box their father built, on an island he stood
     on. Neither of them had seen the notation before that night. One detail
     neither could have invented.
  4. The tide, keeping its own clock: water over the low point already,
     shins, then knees; the run back across the bar in the last light,
     Caleb finding the firm line, Nora calling the time, laughing and
     terrified, a bookend to Chapter 1 with no adults to be right for.
- **Character:** the two of them as a unit for the first time on their own
  account; young, reckless, correct.
- **Creature/world:** the island at dusk, the ants, the logger's tiny green
  light in the dark box.
- **Mysteries:** M04 (advanced: a confirmed real detail). M34 (advanced: the
  logger runs; someone tends it). Proposed M33 (advanced).
- **Continuity:** the card photographed, left in place; the twins wet to the
  thighs and home late; Rachel waiting up.
- **Ending type:** revelation.
- **Hands off:** Mini-Arc 2 — the journal in the boxes is in the same hand.

mid-November, Mini-Arc 2), then a removable brace through the winter, brace off
in March at Ch. 31, wrist weak and cold-shy for months after. Mini-Arcs 2–4
