mode's captions. A translator (person or Claude) uses these and adds to them; a term
changes here first, then everywhere. Names of people never change.

The audio stays English: the voices are English. These are the words on the page and in
the captions.

## Voice

Close third person, past tense, as the English. Dialogue reads as people speak: Jack and
Sarah to each other *aku / kamu*; colleagues *aku / kamu* once they are working together
(Lena, Mark); Mark's first words to Jack, a stranger he calls "Doctor", *saya / Anda*.
Short lines stay short. English idioms are carried by their meaning, not their words
("Try harder." → "Berusahalah lebih keras." reads stiff; "Lebih lucu lagi, dong." keeps the
tease). Numbers are written as words, as the English writes them.

From Chapter 11: Mark, Aiden and Lena with each other *aku / kamu*; Mark to residents
*saya* and "Pak". Doctor Mercer *saya / Anda* to everyone, and Jack and Sarah answer her
with *saya*. Paul Harlan *saya / Anda* to Jack. Jack on the public speakers *saya*, and
the room *Anda*.
