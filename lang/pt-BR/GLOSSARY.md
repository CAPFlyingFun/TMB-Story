# Português (Brasil) — glossary

The story's own words in Português (Brasil), fixed so they read the same in every chapter and in the
Watch mode's captions. A translator (person or Claude) uses these and adds to them; a term
changes here first, then everywhere. Names of people never change: Jack, Sarah, Lena Ortiz,
Mark Jones, Aiden, Doctor Mercer.

The audio stays English. These are the words on the page and in the captions. A translation
says what the English says and nothing more: it never explains, adds or reveals.

## Voice

Brazilian Portuguese, natural and modern, as a good Brazilian novel translation reads -- never European Portuguese. Narration in the past (pretérito perfeito / imperfeito), close third person.

- Jack and Sarah (married): *você*, warm and informal.
- Colleagues working together (Lena, Aiden, Doctor Mercer with Jack and Sarah): *você*.
- Mark's first words to Jack, a stranger he calls "Doctor": *o senhor* ("O senhor é o Doutor Bennett?"); once they are working together (from Chapter 5's ride on), *você*.
- Contractions of speech are welcome in dialogue (*tá*, *pra*) where a Brazilian would say them, sparingly; never in narration.
- Idioms by meaning. Numbers written as words, as the English does.
- Paul Harlan and Jack (from Chapter 13): *o senhor* both ways, Paul's "Doctor" kept as *Doutor*; Paul with Lena and the others: *você*. Residents to Mark/Mercer: *senhor/senhora*. Unit Four's "Yes, sir." = "Sim, senhor."
- Aiden calls Mark "Officer" once, sarcastically: *seu guarda*.
