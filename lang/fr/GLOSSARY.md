# Français — glossary

The story's own words in Français, fixed so they read the same in every chapter and in the
Watch mode's captions. A translator (person or Claude) uses these and adds to them; a term
changes here first, then everywhere. Names of people never change: Jack, Sarah, Lena Ortiz,
Mark Jones, Aiden, Doctor Mercer.

The audio stays English. These are the words on the page and in the captions. A translation
says what the English says and nothing more: it never explains, adds or reveals.

## Voice

Natural modern French, as a good published translation reads. Narration in the passé simple and imparfait (a novel's tense), close third person; dialogue in the passé composé and present as people speak.

- Jack and Sarah (married): *tu*.
- Lena, Aiden and Doctor Mercer with Jack and Sarah (long-standing colleagues): *tu*.
- Mark, security, with Jack and Sarah: *vous* throughout -- he calls Jack "Docteur", and that respect is part of who he is.
- The reader adds straight quotation marks around dialogue lines; inside a line keep straight double quotes where the English has them. Spaces before ? ! : ; follow French usage.
- Idioms by meaning. Numbers written as words, as the English does.
- From Chapter 11: Paul Harlan and the residents with Jack, Sarah and Lena: *vous* (Paul calls Jack "Docteur"). Mark with Aiden and Paul: *vous*; Mark to Unit Four, his junior: *tu*. Doctor Mercer with Mark: *vous*.
