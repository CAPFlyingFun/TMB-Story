# Filipino — glossary

The story's own words in Filipino, fixed so they read the same in every chapter and in the
Watch mode's captions. A translator (person or Claude) uses these and adds to them; a term
changes here first, then everywhere. Names of people never change: Jack, Sarah, Lena Ortiz,
Mark Jones, Aiden, Doctor Mercer.

The audio stays English. These are the words on the page and in the captions. A translation
says what the English says and nothing more: it never explains, adds or reveals.

## Voice

Natural modern Filipino as Filipinos read it today: Tagalog-based, with the English technical words a Filipino engineer or nurse would actually say kept in English (file, console, network, scale factor, boundary event, wrist terminal...). This is how the friends who asked for it talk, and it is far easier to read than invented Tagalog for every technical term. Not heavy Taglish slang either: narration is clean Filipino prose; dialogue may mix in English the way people do.

- Jack and Sarah (married): *ka / ikaw*, warm.
- Colleagues working together: *ka / ikaw*.
- Mark's first words to Jack, a stranger he calls "Doctor" (*Dok*): respectful *po / opo* and *kayo / ninyo*; once they are working together, he may drop *po* with Sarah and Jack but keeps *Dok*.
- Narration in the past (naganap na aspect), close third person.
- Idioms by meaning. Numbers written as words, as the English does (Filipino or English number words, whichever reads naturally; years and times are commonly said in English).
