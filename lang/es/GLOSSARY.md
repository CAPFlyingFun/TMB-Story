# Español — glossary

The story's own words in Español, fixed so they read the same in every chapter and in the
Watch mode's captions. A translator (person or Claude) uses these and adds to them; a term
changes here first, then everywhere. Names of people never change: Jack, Sarah, Lena Ortiz,
Mark Jones, Aiden, Doctor Mercer.

The audio stays English. These are the words on the page and in the captions. A translation
says what the English says and nothing more: it never explains, adds or reveals.

## Voice

Neutral Latin American Spanish: readable from Mexico to Argentina, no *vosotros*, no regional slang. Narration in the past (pretérito / imperfecto), close third person.

- Jack and Sarah (married): *tú*.
- Colleagues working together (Lena, Aiden, Doctor Mercer with Jack and Sarah): *tú*.
- Mark's first words to Jack, a stranger he calls "Doctor": *usted*; once they are working together (from Chapter 5's ride on), *tú*.
- Opening marks ¿ and ¡ always. Dialogue keeps the story's quotation marks (the reader adds them); no em-dash dialogue.
- Idioms by meaning. Numbers written as words, as the English does.
