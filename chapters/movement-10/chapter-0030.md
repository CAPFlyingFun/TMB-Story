# Chapter 30: The Drawings

The archive wasn't on the network.

Lena found that out at noon, when she went looking for it from the station and found nothing but a name in an old directory and a note beside it that said only, terminal, records basement. She called Jack. Jack called Sarah. Sarah called Doctor Mercer before Jack could stop her.

Doctor Mercer's answer came back over the speaker, clipped and tired. "Is there a chair?"

"There's a chair."

"Then sit in it. Four blocks, in a truck, and you sit. If I hear you climbed a stair, I'll come down there myself."

"There are stairs. It's a basement."

There was a long pause on the line.

"Then somebody carries the chair."

Mark drove her the four blocks with the heater on. Jack carried the chair. It was the good one from the kitchen, with arms. He carried it down the narrow concrete stairs of the records building while Sarah came down behind him, one hand on the rail, one hand under her stomach. Every few steps, she told him she was perfectly capable of walking down a staircase.

"I know."

"Then why are you walking backward in front of me?"

"So I can see you."

The basement was cold and dry and smelled of dust. A single long room, shelves of boxed paper down one side, and at the far end, under a plastic cover, a gray metal cabinet with a screen and a keyboard beside it. Lena pulled the cover off. Underneath, the cabinet's one green light was still on.

"It's been running the whole time. Nobody turned it off. Nobody had a reason to turn it on."

Sarah lowered herself into the chair in front of the screen and pulled the keyboard toward her. "What's the password?"

Lena held up a yellowed card that had been taped to the side of the cabinet. "Whoever set it up trusted the basement door."

The screen woke slowly. A plain gray menu, older than anything in the station, with one line on it. Island drawings. Sarah opened it.

The island came up.

Jack had seen photographs of it, a green shape on a gray sea. He had never seen it like this. Thin white lines on a dark blue ground, every road, every slope, every building that had been planned and every one that hadn't been built yet. And across all of it, dividing it into great uneven pieces like the panes of a cracked window, ran heavier lines.

Sarah traced one with her finger. "What are these?"

Lena leaned in. "Seams. I've heard the engineers say it. The island wasn't built in one go. It was built in sections, and they were joined."

She pointed at the label in the corner of one piece. "Modules. That's what the drawings call them."

Mark stood behind them with his arms folded. "And under the seams?"

Sarah zoomed in on one. The heavy line opened up into two thin ones, close together, with a narrow band between them, and the band was labeled. Service gallery.

She zoomed again. A cross section came up, the gallery cut through like a loaf. A long tunnel with a flat floor and a curved roof, and a channel running down the middle of the floor. Along the top of it, at intervals, small squares, each one marked access.

Jack read the note beside the channel out loud. "Surface runoff collection. It's a drain."

Sarah nodded slowly. "Every time it rains on the island, the water has to go somewhere. The ground can't take all of it. So it runs into these. Under every seam."

"And then where?"

She followed the channel along the drawing with her finger. It ran downhill, gently, for a long way. At the low end, the gallery widened into a round chamber, drawn in a darker blue.

Sump.

The darker blue filled it almost to the top.

Nobody said anything for a moment.

Lena's voice was very careful. "That's drawn full. That's what it's meant to look like. That doesn't mean it is."

"No. But it means it was built to hold water."

Sarah sat back in the chair. "Lena, where's the town on this?"

Lena took the keyboard, found a little search box, typed something, and the view slid sideways across the dark blue. A cluster of tiny buildings came into the middle of the screen, small and neat, with the roads running out from them in every direction. Jack knew every one of those roads. On this drawing, they were nothing. A fingerprint on a window.

Mark leaned closer. "That's us?"

"That's us. Before."

Sarah was already looking past it. One of the heavy seam lines ran along the south side of the settlement, not far beyond it. She followed it with her finger to where the southern road ran out toward it, and stopped.

"There."

Just past the end of the southern road, on the seam, sat one of the small squares. Access.

She zoomed in until the square filled the screen. It was a grate. A drawing of a grate, set flat in the ground, with a short shaft under it going straight down into the gallery.

Jack felt his chest go tight. "How close is that to the end of the road?"

Sarah didn't try to measure it on the drawing. She opened the camera feeds instead. The southern road end came up, gray afternoon light on the last stretch of asphalt, the soil beyond it, and the wall of stems behind that.

"If it's there, I should be able to see it."

She moved the camera slowly. Left. A little more. The stems went past, and the soil between them, brown and lumpy and enormous.

Then she stopped.

Down in the soil, between two clumps of grass, half buried in old dead leaves, was a dark shape. Square. And across it, in a row, were bars. Perfectly straight, perfectly even, evenly spaced, dark metal under a skin of dirt.

It was the only straight line out there.

Mark said it very quietly. "That's not the island growing something."

"No."

Sarah didn't take her eyes off it. "That's the island somebody built."

Lena had gone very still. "I've worked at that station a long time. I've never once thought about where the rain went."

Jack looked at the drawing, and then at the camera, and then at the drawing again. A grate. A shaft. A gallery. A sump drawn full of dark blue.

"Can we see down it?"

"Not from here. It's just dark between the bars."

Sarah zoomed in until the picture broke into gray squares, then zoomed back out. "It's past the end of the road, Jack."

"I know."

"Everybody's been told that's the line. Nobody goes past the end of any road. You said it to the whole town."

"I know."

Mark hadn't moved. He was looking at the bars on the screen the way he'd looked at the silk retreat the first morning. Measuring it. Already counting the steps.

"Lena. How old are these drawings?"

"Drawn before most of the town was built."

"So nobody knows what's down there now."

"Nobody knows anything about down there. Nobody's ever needed to."

Mark straightened up. "Then nobody goes anywhere until we do."

Sarah saved the view, and the cross section, and the camera frame, one after another. Then she sat with her hands folded on her stomach and looked at the bars on the screen for a long time.

The light on the old gray cabinet glowed green beside them, steady, as it had glowed every night since the town shrank, with nobody there to see it.
