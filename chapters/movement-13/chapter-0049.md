# Chapter 49: Andy

Nearly there.

Sarah held on to the words the way she was holding on to the end of the bed. She didn't believe them. She held on anyway.

The next one came, and it was not like the others. It didn't just tighten. It pushed. Her whole body bent down around it, and a sound came out of her that she had never made in her life, and when it let go she was gasping, and Doctor Mercer was nodding.

"That's it. That's what we've been waiting for. Next time it comes, you go with it."

"Go with it where?"

"Down. Wherever it wants. You'll know."

Sarah looked at the bed, and the floor, and the wall, and her own hands. Everything in her said not lying down. Not on her back.

"I want to kneel. Up. Against something."

Doctor Mercer didn't argue. She raised the head of the bed until it stood almost upright, and Nurse Hale piled the pillows against it, and Sarah climbed up and knelt facing it, with her arms over the top. Jack stood at the side with both hands on her back. Nurse Hale knelt on the other side with her fingers on the belt.

Doctor Mercer stepped back to look. "Is that where you want to be?"

"That's where I want to be."

"Then that's where we'll do it."

It was not quick. Nobody had told her it would be quick, and it wasn't. Each time, it came, and she pushed, with her chin down and her eyes shut and Doctor Mercer's voice counting low behind her, and each time it went, and she folded down over the pillows, and Jack's hand moved on her back in slow circles, and the fast soft sound went on from the speaker beside the bed. A hundred and forty. A hundred and forty. Every time she heard it, she breathed.

Somewhere in the middle of it, Jack leaned close to her ear.

"For the record, I'd like it noted that I did offer to do this part."

She didn't even open her eyes. "When?"

"Just now. In my head."

"Jack, if you make me laugh right now, I'll never forgive you."

He kept quiet after that. But she felt him smile against her hair, and something in her chest loosened that she hadn't known was tight.

An hour. More. The light around the blind grew brighter. Her arms shook, and then her legs. Once, between two of them, she thought about the pain medicine, really thought about it, and then remembered what Doctor Mercer had said it would cost.

"I don't want the medicine. You said it costs strength at the end. I want the end."

Doctor Mercer nodded. "It's too close for it now anyway. You don't need it. You're doing it."

"Can I see? Is there a mirror?"

There was. Nurse Hale had it ready, as if she had known all along. She held it low and angled it, and Sarah looked down past her own shaking arms into it, and saw.

Dark hair. Wet and dark, and there.

"Oh."

She laughed, and it came out of her with a sob in it. "Jack. Jack, he's got hair."

Jack leaned down to look, and his breath caught, and he didn't say anything at all.

Doctor Mercer's voice was very calm and very close. "Next one, Sarah. This is the one. Not too fast. Breathe him out. I've got him."

It came.

She pushed, and pushed.

"Breathe. Breathe. Little ones now. Little ones."

She breathed in small hard pants, and there was a long burning moment that she would never be able to describe to anyone afterward, and then there was a rush, and a slide, and a weight that was suddenly gone, and Doctor Mercer's hands moving.

And he cried, almost before she knew he was out.

It was loud. It was furious. It filled the whole room, and the corridor, and probably half the town.

"There he is. There he is. Turn around, Sarah. Slowly. Sit back. Here he comes."

Jack helped her turn, and sit back against the pillows, and Doctor Mercer laid him on her chest.

He was warm and wet and slippery and so small, and so heavy, and he was still crying, red in the face, his fists up, his eyes screwed shut against the light. Nurse Hale laid a warm towel over both of them and rubbed his back through it, firmly, and he cried harder.

"That's what we want. Shout at us. Go on."

Sarah put her hand on his back, over the towel. He was breathing. He was shouting. He was here.

Behind Nurse Hale, against the wall, the warming box hummed to itself with its little light on, and nobody looked at it. The breathing bottle stood on its cart, full to its mark.

Sarah looked at the clock on the wall, and read it twice. Doctor Mercer was already reading it out, for Nurse Hale to write down.

"Twelve minutes to one. May seventh."

Then she did the things she had always meant to do, quietly, at the end of the bed. The afterbirth came the way it should. She gave the injection from the envelope with Sarah's name on it, the way she had said she would, back when she had read out her list. Nurse Hale checked the bleeding, and looked up.

"Fine. All fine. Just what it should be."

That was all.

Sarah barely noticed. He had stopped crying. He had gone quiet against her, his cheek on her skin, and he had opened his eyes.

They were dark and cloudy, and they couldn't see much farther than her face, Doctor Mercer would tell her later. But they were open, and they were turned toward her face.

Jack was kneeling beside the bed. He had been crying for a while, she realized, without making any sound about it.

"Hello. Hello, you."

He put one finger against the baby's hand, and the hand opened, and closed around it.

Jack laughed, a wet, shaky laugh. "He's got my ears."

"He's got two of them. I counted."

"They stick out."

"They're perfect."

Jack got up then and went to the bag in the corner, and knelt down, and dug all the way to the bottom of it. When he came back he had Mr. Pell's wife's blanket in his hands, white and soft and knitted in its little raised squares, the one made years ago for a grandchild nobody got.

He held it up, and his voice shook. "You said we wouldn't need it until the end."

He unfolded it, and laid it over the towel, over both of them, and tucked it in.

Sarah looked down at the face against her chest. The dark wet hair, the small frowning mouth, the ears.

She had known for weeks. She had wanted to see him first, to be sure it was his. She looked at him, and it was.

"Andy."

Jack went very still beside her. Then he said it slowly, as if he were trying it in his mouth. "Andy."

Then softer, to the baby. "Andy. Hello, Andy."

The baby yawned, all the way, as if the whole business had been very tiring for him, and went to sleep.

On Jack's wrist, something chimed. He lifted it, and Sarah saw the name on it, Mark, and one word under it. Anything? Jack looked at it for a long moment, and laughed under his breath, because the whole building must have heard. Then he lifted the wrist to his mouth.

"Yes."

From the far end of the corridor, by the front door, where Mark had apparently been standing for an hour, there was a silence. Then a long breath over the wrist.

"Copy."

Doctor Mercer lifted him onto the little scale for a moment, and he complained about it, and she gave him straight back. Then she wrote one more thing down. "Three and a half kilos, near enough. Ten fingers. Ten toes."

She closed the book and looked at the three of them, and for the first time since Sarah had known her, she smiled with her whole face.

"Well done. All of you."

Sarah put her head back against the pillow, and her hand on Andy's back, and felt him breathe.

Through the blind, far off beyond the edge of the town, the robin was still singing. She listened to it for a while. Then she looked down at him again, asleep on her chest under the old white blanket, in a town that had been made very small.

Andy Bennett. She said it to herself, once, to be sure.

It was his.
