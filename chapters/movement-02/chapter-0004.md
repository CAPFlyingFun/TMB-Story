# Chapter 4: The First Calls

The first call arrived before Jack could let go of Sarah's hand.

His wrist terminal vibrated once against his skin. Not an emergency cascade. Not a settlement alarm. One incoming call from island utility control.

For several seconds, the impossible forest beyond the settlement remained perfectly indifferent.

Sarah looked past Jack toward the darkened homes outside. Most of their windows were still black.

"They don't know," she said.

Jack understood immediately. Nearly five hundred people had just been carried into a different scale, and most of them were still asleep.

He answered the call. "Bennett."

Lena's voice came through quietly. "Jack, what happened?"

He turned toward the window. A blade of grass swayed beyond the streetlights, its tip disappearing into the darkness above the nearest buildings.

"TOMBS activated," Jack said. "We think the developed zone was inside the boundary."

Lena paused. "Think?"

"The system hid the final coordinates before activation." Jack looked at the event record. "We only confirmed the result afterward."

"And the result is?"

Jack pulled the scale factor back onto the console. The answer still sounded absurd.

"It changed our scale."

Silence filled the call.

Sarah stepped beside him. "Lena, go to a window. Don't go outside."

Jack heard a chair roll, followed by quick footsteps and the hiss of an interior door.

Then Lena stopped moving.

"Oh."

Jack brought up the utility station's exterior cameras. "What do you see?"

"I'm not sure." Her voice had gone very still. "There's a wall behind the east fence."

Sarah leaned toward Jack's screen as he selected the camera. The utility station appeared intact beneath its exterior lights. Transformers, service vehicles, and the perimeter fence all looked exactly as they had before the activation.

Beyond the fence stood a wall of brown earth.

Roots curled through it like heavy cables. Pebbles protruded from the surface, except one pebble appeared nearly as tall as the utility building.

Jack enlarged the feed. "That's the ground."

Lena let out a quiet breath. "I was hoping you wouldn't say that."

"Stay inside," Jack said. "Keep the night crew inside too. No general alarm yet. I don't want people pouring into the streets before we know what's out there."

"Already locking the exterior doors."

Lena continued, steadier now. "Most of the developed zone still has power. Three localized faults, nothing catastrophic."

"That's good," Jack said.

"It is," Lena said.

Jack stared at the dark residential blocks on the map. "It doesn't feel good."

A message appeared from settlement security.

SOUTH PATROL: ROAD ENDS AT PERIMETER. REQUEST INSTRUCTIONS.

Jack opened the channel. "Unit Twelve, this is Bennett. Do not cross the end of the road. Turn around and return to the developed zone."

A man's voice answered. "Doctor, I wasn't planning on crossing it."

"What do you see?"

"Dirt. Grass. A lot of both." The officer hesitated. "And I think the dirt is higher than my vehicle."

Sarah leaned closer to the speaker. "Any movement?"

"Negative. Just... big."

Jack almost smiled at the inadequacy of the word. "Return to town. Quietly. Security personnel only at the perimeter until we know more."

"Understood."

The channel closed.

For the next several minutes, the settlement remained mostly asleep.

That was the strangest part.

No sirens. No crowds. No screaming in the streets. Porch lights appeared one at a time as light sleepers investigated the vibration. A bedroom window opened in one housing block and closed again. Somewhere farther away, a dog began barking.

Sarah watched the scattered lights appear. "We can't keep this quiet until morning."

"I know."

"But waking everyone right now could be worse."

Jack looked toward the impossible grass beyond the streetlights. "Then we tell the people who need to know first. Medical. Utility. Security. Anyone already awake."

"And everyone else?"

"We give ourselves enough time to learn what we're waking them up to."

Sarah nodded slowly. "Not long."

"No."

They turned back to the consoles.

They divided the screens between them. Sarah began organizing incoming reports by medical, structural, and utility priority while Jack opened the settlement's infrastructure schematic.

Power lines crossed the developed zone in a web of glowing paths. Water, drainage, communications, and service conduits followed.

Several lines ended precisely at the TOMBS boundary.

Jack leaned closer.

Sarah noticed his expression. "What?"

"The external connections."

She moved to his side.

The settlement's main freshwater intake ran toward a reservoir outside the developed zone. On the schematic, the line remained visible until the red boundary. Beyond it, the monitoring system showed nothing.

Sarah traced the route with one finger. "Could the sensors just be disconnected?"

"Maybe."

Jack selected a maintenance camera near the eastern service corridor.

The pipe emerged from beneath a utility building and crossed several meters of developed ground. It should have continued through the boundary toward the reservoir.

Instead, it ended.

The exposed end was smooth.

No torn metal. No crushed fittings. No water spraying into the service corridor.

It looked manufactured that way.

Sarah leaned closer. "Where's the rest of it?"

Jack switched camera angles. "I don't know."

Lena's call returned through Sarah's tablet. "You found the intake, didn't you?"

Sarah picked it up. "We're looking at it."

"I checked three boundary crossings," Lena said. "Same thing. Power conduit, water intake, and the old fiber trunk. Everything ends cleanly."

Jack opened the utility inventory. "How much water is stored inside the settlement?"

"I'm calculating consumption now." Lena paused as keys clicked faintly through the call. "The tanks were designed as buffers, not as our only supply."

Sarah opened the population estimate. "Give us the conservative number when you have it."

"I will."

Jack stared at the clean end of the pipe.

The boundary had not simply cut through the settlement.

It had decided what belonged inside it.

A low vibration passed through the floor.

Jack's head lifted.

Sarah stopped typing.

The vibration faded, then returned several seconds later, deeper than before. A loose pen rolled across the console and tapped against Jack's hand.

Sarah rested one palm against her stomach.

Jack turned immediately. "You okay?"

"Yes." She waited, then gave him a small nod. "He's moving."

Jack let out the breath he had started holding.

Lena's voice came through the tablet. "Please tell me that was something in your building."

Jack checked the structural sensors. No seismic activity. No machinery had restarted.

"It wasn't."

He reopened the utility camera.

Dust trickled down the enormous soil face beyond the eastern fence.

Another vibration rolled through the settlement.

This time, something moved outside the camera light.

Jack froze the feed and stepped through the frames one at a time. There was no clear shape, only a shadow crossing the illuminated soil from one side to the other.

It was gone in less than a second.

Sarah stared at the screen. "Could that be a branch?"

"Maybe."

The ground trembled again.

On the camera, a pebble shifted and rolled down the dirt wall. It struck the earth beside the fence hard enough to throw dust across the service road.

At their new scale, the pebble looked like a boulder.

Lena spoke more quietly. "Jack?"

He kept his eyes on the dark edge of the feed.

"Nobody goes outside the developed zone," he said.

Sarah looked toward the impossible forest beyond the window.

This time, she didn't argue.

Around midnight, most of the settlement was still asleep. Inside the laboratory, the past hour had felt endless. Outside, it had almost gone unnoticed.

Jack stood at the main console with a cup of coffee he had forgotten to drink. A handful of porch lights had come on since the last vibration, scattered across the residential blocks like the town was blinking awake one eye at a time. Everything else stayed dark: streets empty, windows black, the settlement behaving as though nothing at all had happened while it slept through the strangest hour of its existence.

Only a few people knew otherwise. Utility. Security. A handful of night-shift workers who had felt the floor move and gone to a window instead of back to bed.

For now, that was small enough to manage.

Medical remained stable. Utility crews had isolated the few damaged lines they could reach safely. The settlement still had power, breathable air, and stored water.

It was an impressive list of things that had not gone wrong.

Jack hated that it felt reassuring.

Sarah sat at the neighboring console with her feet propped on a small equipment case. She had objected when Jack brought it over, then used it without another word.

He glanced at her.

Sarah caught him looking. "Don't."

"I didn't say anything."

"You were about to."

Jack returned to his screen. "I was thinking internally."

"With your eyes open this time?"

"Advanced technique."

Sarah's mouth twitched, but she didn't look up from her tablet.

A call came through on the console. Lena's name.

"We have a water estimate," she said, before Jack could even greet her.

Jack straightened. "How bad?"

"At current normal consumption, bad. With emergency restrictions, considerably less bad."

Sarah lowered her tablet. "How long?"

"Several days before we have to start making uncomfortable decisions. Longer if we shut down nonessential industrial use and recycle aggressively."

Jack nodded. "Do it."

"Already started."

Sarah glanced at him. "I like her."

"So do I."

Lena's voice came through dryly. "I can hear you."

Jack muted the channel.

Sarah laughed quietly.

For a moment, it almost sounded like an ordinary late shift.
