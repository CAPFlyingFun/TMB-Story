# Chapter 6: Someone Knew

Jack watched the southern camera feed all the way back to the laboratory.

Nothing followed them onto the road.

That should have made him feel better.

It didn't.

The shape had disappeared before he could identify it, and the security cameras were too low to see over the ridge of soil. Every few seconds, another grass stem moved beyond the boundary.

Sarah was waiting in the control room when Jack returned.

She crossed the room before the door had fully opened and stopped directly in front of him. Her eyes moved over his face, arms, and clothing.

Jack held out both hands. "Still attached."

Sarah touched his chest anyway. "You said thin legs."

"I said I saw something that looked like thin legs."

"How many?"

"I don't know."

"How big?"

"I don't know."

Sarah stared at him.

Jack lowered his voice. "That's why we left."

Some of the tension left her shoulders.

"Good."

Lena remained at the console with the southern camera enlarged. "For the record, I support running away from unidentified giant things."

Jack walked over. "We didn't run."

One of the security officers entered behind him. "We ran."

Lena pointed at the officer. "I like him."

Jack ignored both of them and connected the environmental sensor to the console.

Sarah moved beside him. "Anything useful?"

"Everything outside the boundary tested normal."

"Normal for us?"

Jack shook his head. "Normal atmosphere. Same composition, same pressure, same temperature within the sensor's tolerance."

Lena frowned. "Then physically, we can breathe out there."

"Probably."

Sarah looked at him. "Probably isn't going in the public announcement either."

"No."

Jack uploaded the wrist-camera recording from the perimeter. The image of the enormous fingernail clipping appeared on the main screen.

Lena stared at it. "I hate that."

"Everyone hates that."

"Good. I wanted to make sure I was contributing."

Jack advanced the recording until the grass began moving. The image shook as he returned to the vehicle.

He slowed the footage.

Frame by frame, darkness shifted between the stems.

Then a shape appeared.

Jack stopped.

Sarah leaned closer.

The image was blurred, but several narrow forms crossed the gap. One touched the ground before the rest vanished behind the ridge.

Lena tilted her head. "Leg?"

"Maybe."

Sarah pointed to the upper portion of the frame. "Go back."

Jack reversed several frames.

"Stop."

He froze the image.

A curved shape hung above the leg, faintly reflecting the security lights.

Jack enlarged it until the pixels began breaking apart.

Lena asked, "What is it?"

Sarah's eyes stayed on the screen. "Could be an insect."

Nobody answered.

Jack had known that possibility from the moment he saw the legs.

Hearing Sarah say it made the room colder.

Lena looked toward the window. "How big are we?"

Jack closed the recording.

"Small enough."

"That is not a measurement."

"It's the one I have right now."

Sarah pulled up the scale factor from the TOMBS event log. "We can calculate it."

Jack looked at the number again.

The scale factor had bothered him since the activation, but not only because of what it represented.

Something about it was too precise.

He opened the original TOMBS calibration records.

Sarah noticed the new window. "What are you looking for?"

"The scale target."

"You already have it."

"I have the result."

Jack searched through archived test configurations. TOMBS had been designed to accept a target scale or a relative transformation factor, calculate field requirements, verify mass and boundary stability, then request authorization before activation.

Tonight it had skipped the last part.

Jack opened the unauthorized command sequence.

Lena stepped closer. "Can you tell what they entered?"

"Most of it was scrubbed."

"Most?"

Jack highlighted a surviving parameter block.

Sarah read it first. "Target height normalization."

Jack nodded.

Lena looked between them. "In normal-person language?"

Sarah answered while Jack expanded the field. "TOMBS wasn't simply told to make the settlement smaller."

"It was given a target scale," Jack said.

Lena's expression changed. "Meaning whoever did this chose how small?"

"That's what I'm trying to confirm."

Jack compared the command with TOMBS's internal calculation.

A value appeared.

He stared at it.

Sarah leaned toward him. "What?"

Jack ran the conversion again.

Same result.

He entered an average adult height into a separate calculator and applied the scale.

Sarah watched the answer appear.

"Jack."

"I know."

Lena moved closer. "What does it say?"

Jack looked at his own hands.

"If the transformation is uniform, an average adult here is somewhere around several millimeters tall."

Lena blinked. "Several?"

"Roughly."

Sarah placed a hand against her stomach.

Jack noticed immediately, but she shook her head before he could ask.

"I'm fine. Keep going."

He turned back to the calculation.

The fingernail clipping made sense.

The water droplet made sense.

The grass made sense.

So did the thing moving outside.

Jack wished fewer things made sense.

Jack's wrist terminal chirped with an incoming call.

MEDICAL CENTER.

Jack glanced at Sarah before answering. "Bennett."

"Doctor Bennett, this is Doctor Mercer." A woman's voice, brisk and fully awake. "Did your lab cause that vibration a little while ago?"

"Probably."

"That is not the answer I wanted."

"It's not the one I wanted to give." Jack pulled up the medical center's status panel. "Any damage?"

"Nothing serious. One patient woke up frightened, two nurses felt the floor move, and half my overnight staff are arguing about whether we had an earthquake."

Jack checked the structural grid. "Local sensors show no seismic event."

Doctor Mercer was quiet for a beat. "Then why am I looking at grass taller than the medical center?"

Jack closed his eyes briefly.

"You looked outside."

"Jack, I work nights. Looking outside occasionally is one of the perks."

Sarah leaned toward the microphone. "I like her."

Jack gave Sarah a look and returned to the call. "We had a TOMBS boundary event. The developed settlement's been reduced in scale relative to the surrounding island. We're still working out by how much."

Doctor Mercer said nothing.

Jack waited.

"Say that again."

"The town got smaller."

Another pause.

"That was not better."

"I know."

Doctor Mercer's voice shifted from disbelief into something steadier, more procedural. "What do you need from medical?"

"For now, keep your overnight staff inside. Check backup power, medical gases, water pressure, and anything that crosses the edge of the developed zone. Don't wake patients unless you need to."

"And when they wake on their own?"

Jack looked toward the dark residential map. "We're working on that."

"Understood." A pause. "Jack. How long before the rest of the settlement knows?"

"Not long," he admitted.

"Then don't wait too long to tell me what to do about it."

The call ended.

Nobody spoke for a moment.

Sarah broke the silence. "Morning's coming."

"I know."

"We can't keep this to Lena, security, and one very unimpressed doctor forever."

Jack rubbed his eyes. "We just need a little more before we lose the choice."

Lena, still watching the frozen frame of the shape in the grass, didn't look up. "For what it's worth, I'd rather know what that is before we wake everyone up to look at it."

"Same," Jack said.

He turned back to the console.

Lena stared at the scale factor. "Could the system have picked that number randomly?"

"No."

"Could it have been an accident?"

Jack hesitated.

Sarah answered more carefully. "The activation itself could have resulted from corrupted commands. But a specific target scale is different."

Jack opened the boundary definition.

The red outline appeared around the developed settlement again.

Research buildings.

Homes.

Medical.

Utilities.

Workshops.

Roads.

Almost nothing beyond them.

He overlaid the island development survey beneath it.

The match was nearly exact.

Sarah folded her arms. "The boundary wasn't centered on TOMBS."

"No."

"It wasn't a radius."

"No."

Lena studied the irregular shape. "It follows the town."

Jack nodded.

The room fell quiet.

The boundary curved around a maintenance yard on the north side, extended outward to include a small pumping station, then pulled inward to avoid a stretch of undeveloped woodland.

That was not how an uncontrolled field expanded.

It was how a person drew a selection.

Sarah pointed toward the pumping station. "Why include that?"

Jack opened its infrastructure record. "Wastewater processing."

Lena pointed to another narrow extension. "And that?"

"Backup substation."

Sarah looked at him.

Jack zoomed out.

The boundary had captured almost everything the settlement would need to remain alive for the first few hours.

But not everything it would need forever.

Water storage had come with them.

The reservoir had not.

Power generation inside the developed zone had come with them.

Several external resources had not.

Medical had come with them.

The wilderness had not.

Jack felt a knot form in his stomach.

Lena spoke first. "Somebody knew the layout."

Sarah shook her head slowly. "More than the layout."

Jack looked at her.

"They knew what counted as developed infrastructure."

He returned to the access records from before the activation. The unidentified connection had appeared and disappeared. Security history had been cleaned. His credentials had been revoked.

Someone had entered TOMBS remotely.

Someone had selected the settlement.

Someone had chosen a scale.

And whoever had done it had known enough about the island to draw the boundary around the systems keeping five hundred people alive.

Jack opened the raw command archive.

"There's another copy."

Sarah moved closer. "Of what?"

"The activation sequence."

He pointed to a maintenance partition buried beneath the main event log. TOMBS automatically stored a low-level diagnostic trace during every field calculation. It was intended for hardware troubleshooting, not security review.

The intruder had erased the normal logs.

They had not erased this.

Jack opened the file.

Lines of machine data filled the screen.

Lena pulled over a chair. "Can you read that?"

"Eventually."

Sarah sat beside him. "Search for the boundary coordinates."

Jack did.

The system found several matches.

He narrowed the results to the earliest occurrence.

A timestamp appeared.

Jack stopped breathing.

Sarah noticed. "What?"

He checked it again.

The boundary definition had not been created during the attack.

It had been loaded.

Lena leaned over the back of Jack's chair. "Loaded from where?"

Jack opened the file metadata.

The creation time appeared.

Sarah read it aloud. "Three weeks ago."

Nobody moved.

Jack checked the timestamp against the island clock, then against the TOMBS backup server.

Same date.

Same time.

Sarah's voice dropped. "Could that be wrong?"

"Not on both systems."

Lena looked at the boundary map and then back at the timestamp. "So someone prepared this three weeks ago?"

Jack didn't answer yet.

He searched the project directory for the file name.

Nothing.

He searched the backup.

Nothing.

Then he searched the archived maintenance snapshots.

One result appeared.

Jack opened it.

The file existed for less than a minute in the snapshot before being deleted.

Sarah leaned closer. "Can you see who created it?"

Jack opened the ownership field.

His name appeared.

He stared at the screen.

Lena looked at him. "Jack?"

Sarah immediately shook her head. "He didn't."

"I know," Lena said. "I'm asking why the computer thinks he did."

Jack opened the authentication signature.

His administrator certificate.

His credentials.

His project key.

All valid.

All recorded three weeks before the attack.

Sarah moved her chair closer to him. "Where were you?"

"Three weeks ago?"

"At that time."

Jack searched his calendar, then stopped.

He remembered before the entry loaded.

Sarah saw it in his face. "What?"

"I wasn't in the laboratory."

The calendar appeared.

Jack had been across the island at a settlement planning meeting.

Sarah had been there too.

Lena read the entry. "You were both at the community center."

"For almost two hours," Sarah said.

Jack looked back at the file.

His credentials had created a boundary definition while he was nowhere near TOMBS.

He opened the access history from that day.

Clean.

Too clean.

The same way tonight's records had been clean.

Sarah placed her hand on the edge of the console. "This didn't start tonight."

"No."

Jack searched the diagnostic snapshot for anything else associated with the file.

A short text field appeared beneath the boundary definition.

Not a command.

Not part of TOMBS's normal configuration.

A label.

Jack opened it.

Three words appeared on the screen.

PHASE ONE READY.

Lena went still.

Sarah stared at the message. "Phase one?"

Jack read it again.

Outside, something struck the distant ground.

The control-room window trembled.

Nobody looked away from the screen.

Jack had spent the last couple of hours trying to understand what had gone wrong with TOMBS.

Now, for the first time, he understood something worse.

It had gone exactly the way someone planned.
