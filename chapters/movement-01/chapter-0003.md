# Chapter 3: The Activation

Jack's fingers raced over the keyboard. "Give me manual control."

"Access denied." He tried a second route. "Come on. Give me manual control!"

"Access denied." Sarah moved to the secondary console. "I'll try to interrupt the calculation."

Jack nodded toward her console. "Do it."

Her fingers moved quickly across the controls. "Nothing."

Jack opened another diagnostic path. "Again."

Sarah tried the command a second time. "I am."

The building shook hard enough that Sarah caught herself against the console. Jack immediately grabbed her arm. "You okay?"

Sarah steadied herself against the console. "I'm fine."

Jack kept hold of her arm. "Sarah."

She met his eyes. "I'm fine. Keep working."

Another vibration rolled through the floor. The muted warning tones at the consoles cut off one by one. Jack's screen flashed.

"Scale factor locked." Sarah stared at the value. "What does that mean?"

Jack didn't answer.

Sarah stepped closer. "Jack?"

Jack finally looked away from the screen. "That's not possible."

Sarah pointed at the locked value. "What does it mean?"

Before he could respond, every light in the laboratory turned white.

Sarah grabbed his hand. "Jack!"

The sound vanished. It didn't fade or become muffled. It simply ceased to exist.

Jack could see Sarah shouting, but he couldn't hear her. The laboratory seemed to stretch around them, the walls impossibly far away for a fraction of a second and then impossibly close. His stomach dropped as light bent around Sarah's body.

Jack tightened his grip on her hand.

The floor seemed to disappear beneath his feet.

Then everything snapped back.

Sound returned all at once as Jack hit the floor and Sarah landed beside him. Somewhere inside the array chamber, metal rang once and went still.

Jack crawled toward her. "Sarah! Are you hurt?"

Sarah pushed herself upright. "I'm here. I don't think so."

Jack looked at her stomach. "The baby?"

Sarah pressed a hand against her stomach. "Give me a second."

Jack searched her face. "Sarah?"

She closed her eyes, waited, and then smiled. "He moved."

Jack released the breath he had been holding. "Okay."

Sarah squeezed his hand. "We're okay."

The laboratory settled into an unsettling silence. Jack slowly stood and looked around. Nothing appeared different. The consoles were intact, the TOMBS Array had stopped, and even the emergency lighting had returned to normal.

Sarah got to her feet beside him and looked around. "Did it fail?"

Jack glanced back at the main console. The display had gone dark except for a single status light. He crossed the room and touched the controls. "TOMBS is offline."

Sarah followed more carefully, one hand resting beneath her stomach. "Offline because you stopped it?"

Jack tried the controls again. He hated how quickly the answer came. "No. Offline because it finished."

Sarah looked toward the chamber window. "Finished what?"

Jack pulled up the local sensor grid. Temperature, pressure, and atmosphere all looked normal. Structural sensors showed no collapse. The laboratory had power. The floor was still beneath them. On paper, almost nothing had happened.

That bothered him more than a room full of broken equipment would have.

Sarah watched him move through the readings. "Anything?"

Jack opened another panel. "Everything says we're fine. Which means something is very wrong."

Jack looked through the chamber window. "I don't know."

His wrist terminal chirped with an incoming call from island utility control. Jack answered, and Lena's voice burst through it. "Jack! Jack, answer me!"

Jack raised his wrist. "I'm here."

Lena spoke quickly. "Something happened."

Jack brought up the building status. "I know. Status?"

Lena checked the system report from the utility station. "We've got power. Most systems are responding, but communications are down."

Jack opened the communications panel. "Internal?"

Lena answered, "External. We can't reach anything beyond the settlement."

Jack selected the satellite link. "Satellite?"

Lena replied, "Nothing."

Jack switched to radio. "Radio?"

Lena answered again. "Nothing."

Behind him, Sarah had walked toward a window. "Jack."

Jack kept his attention on the terminal. "Try the emergency frequencies."

Sarah called again from the window. "Jack."

Jack cycled through another channel. "One second."

Sarah's voice tightened. "Jack, come here."

Something in her voice made him lower his wrist. Sarah stood motionless at the window.

Jack walked over and stopped beside her. "What is it?"

She didn't answer.

He looked outside.

For several seconds, his mind refused to understand what he was seeing. The street was there. The building across from them was there. Streetlights still glowed, and a parked utility vehicle sat exactly where it had been.

Everything inside the settlement looked normal.

Beyond it, the world was wrong.

Jack stepped closer to the glass. "What..."

The hills beyond the research district were gone. In their place stood enormous shapes reaching into the darkness.

Sarah pointed toward the edge of the settlement. "Is that... grass?"

Jack stared at a blade rising beyond the developed area. It wasn't inches high or even feet high from where they stood. It towered above buildings. Another stood beside it, then another, until thousands disappeared into the night like a forest.

Jack backed away from the window. "No."

Sarah turned toward him. "Jack, what did TOMBS do?"

He rushed to the console and opened the camera controls. "External cameras."

Jack opened the settlement's perimeter feeds. Camera One showed the northern edge buried beneath towering grass. Camera Two showed the western road ending at an enormous wall of earth and vegetation. Camera Three showed a tree Jack had walked past hundreds of times.

Now the camera couldn't fit its trunk on the screen.

Jack switched the feed to a camera mounted higher on the communications building and rotated it toward the coastline. The camera moved normally, but the horizon never appeared. Grass stems, leaves, and dark walls of soil filled the view in every direction.

Sarah leaned closer to the screen. "Can you zoom out?"

"It is zoomed out."

She looked at him, then back at the image.

Jack changed cameras again. A security light near the southern boundary illuminated what looked like a curved glass wall. For a moment he could not place it. Then a bead of water slid down the surface, trembling in the light.

Sarah whispered, "That's a drop of water."

Jack said nothing. The droplet was enormous compared with the nearby road.

A faint vibration passed through the building. It was softer than the TOMBS Array had been, but deeper, almost like distant thunder. Both of them froze.

"Was that us?" Sarah asked.

Jack checked the structural monitor. "No seismic event."

The vibration came again, then faded into the night.

Sarah looked toward the window. "Then what was it?"

Jack did not answer. He was no longer certain he wanted to know.

Sarah came up beside him and stared at the feeds. "The town..."

Jack opened the TOMBS event log. One final record remained.

"Boundary event complete." Below it was the scale factor.

Jack stared at the number.

Sarah covered her mouth. "No."

The computer calmly displayed what neither of them was ready to say. The island hadn't grown. The wilderness hadn't changed. The ocean hadn't changed. The rest of the world was exactly the same size it had been ten minutes ago.

Only the settlement was different.

Jack opened the boundary record and overlaid it on the settlement map. The line traced the research district, housing blocks, medical center, workshops, utilities, and the streets connecting them. It stopped almost perfectly where the developed town gave way to untouched island.

Sarah stared at the outline. "It took all of us."

Jack swallowed. "Everyone inside the boundary."

"The homes?"

"Yes."

"The medical center?"

"Yes."

Sarah looked down at her stomach and then toward the darkened town beyond the glass. "Five hundred people."

Jack nodded once. The number felt different now. It was no longer a population statistic in a planning report. It was every sleeping family, every night-shift technician, every child, every patient, and every person who would wake to a world that no longer fit them.

Jack looked through the window again at the impossible forest beyond the streetlights. Five hundred people, their homes, laboratories, vehicles, and everything else inside the TOMBS boundary had been caught in the same event.

Sarah reached for his hand. "Jack..."

He squeezed her fingers. For the first time since the alarm sounded, he had no answer.

Their town had shrunk.

And the island had not.
