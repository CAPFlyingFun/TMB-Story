# Chapter 2: The Boundary

The lights flickered as a low hum passed through the floor. Sarah turned toward the far end of the laboratory. "Was that the array?"

Jack was already out of his chair. "Stay here."

Sarah stood and picked up her tablet. "Absolutely not."

She folded her arms. "Being thirty-two weeks pregnant does not mean made of glass."

Jack lowered his voice. "I know."

Sarah nodded once. "Good."

Jack glanced at her stomach and then met her eyes. "It means you're carrying something considerably more important than this laboratory."

Her expression softened. "And you're his father."

Jack opened his mouth, but Sarah pointed down the corridor. "So we're both going."

Jack gave in and opened the door wider. "Fine. But stay with me."

Sarah fell into step beside him. "That I can do."

They hurried toward the main control room while another alarm sounded overhead.

"Warning. array power above standby threshold." Jack tapped his wrist terminal and called the island utility station. "Control, this is Bennett. Shut down power to TOMBS."

Lena Ortiz, the night-shift power technician, answered through his wrist terminal almost immediately. "Jack, we were about to call you. We're seeing a massive draw from your building."

Jack quickened his pace. "How massive?"

Lena checked the readout on her end. "Fourteen megawatts and climbing."

Jack stopped so abruptly that Sarah nearly walked into him. "Fourteen?"

Lena came back immediately. "Fifteen now."

Sarah checked the figures on her tablet. "Yesterday's full test didn't use half that."

Jack started moving again. "I know."

Lena's voice returned through Jack's terminal. "What's happening over there?"

"Possible network intrusion. Cut external access to the laboratory and disconnect TOMBS from the island grid."

"Doing it now."

The corridor lights dimmed. For a moment, the building became almost silent, but the lights returned and the hum beneath their feet continued.

Jack's wrist terminal chirped again. "Jack? We disconnected you."

Jack looked toward the array chamber. "It's still running."

Lena went quiet for a beat. "That's impossible."

Jack headed toward the array chamber. "Tonight's developing a theme."

Sarah caught Jack's arm as they resumed walking. "Backup capacitors?"

Jack shook his head. "Not enough for this."

Sarah checked another possibility. "Internal reactor?"

Jack tapped the reactor status on his wrist display. "Offline."

Sarah looked toward the humming chamber. "Then where is it getting the power?"

Jack shook his head. "I don't know."

They reached the main control room, where a reinforced window looked into the chamber beyond. At its center stood the TOMBS Array, the Temporal Object Manipulation and Boundary System. Several articulated rings surrounded a central platform. Under normal operation, the rings positioned themselves around a target while the system established a controlled boundary.

Tonight, every ring was moving.

"Jack." Sarah's voice dropped as she stared through the reinforced window.

"I see it."

Sarah turned from the rings to him. "You didn't authorize an experiment?"

Jack moved toward the primary console. "No."

The largest ring rotated into position while another slid sideways. Sarah moved to the console. "Boundary emitters are active."

Jack pointed to the emitter controls. "Kill them."

Sarah worked the controls and shook her head. "They're not responding."

Jack opened the emergency panel. "Then we'll do it the old-fashioned way." He pulled the physical shutdown lever, and the room went dark.

The rings stopped.

Sarah exhaled and loosened her grip on the console. "Okay."

Jack kept his hand on the shutdown lever. "That worked."

A light blinked inside the chamber, then another. The rings began moving again.

Sarah took a step backward. "Jack."

Jack checked the lever and its indicator. "I cut the physical connection."

Sarah watched the rings accelerate. "Apparently TOMBS disagrees."

Jack moved back toward the console. "Machines don't get opinions."

Sarah followed him. "This one seems pretty committed."

The emergency lights switched on. Jack rushed back to the console, which should have been dead. Instead, the screen was filling with data.

"Boundary acquisition in progress." Sarah leaned closer to the new warning. "What boundary?"

Jack opened the target parameters. "That's wrong."

Sarah moved beside him. "What?"

Jack highlighted the figures. "The target dimensions."

Sarah leaned closer. The values flickered, vanished, and returned as unreadable placeholders.

"It just hid them," she said.

Jack tried to reopen the raw parameters. "Or somebody hid them."

Sarah pulled up the mapping controls. A red outline appeared for less than a second, too fast to resolve, then the map cleared itself.

Jack stared at the empty display. "Do that again."

"I didn't do it the first time." Sarah tried the command anyway. "Mapping access denied."

Jack opened a second diagnostic path. The system returned only one line.

"Boundary acquisition in progress."

Sarah looked toward the array chamber. "Boundary around what?"

"That's what I want to know." Jack keyed his wrist terminal. "Lena, keep the laboratory isolated. Don't sound a settlement alarm yet."

Lena answered immediately. "You sure?"

"No. But I don't know what we'd be telling people to do, and I don't want anyone running toward whatever this is."

Sarah nodded. "Quiet containment until we know the boundary."

"Exactly." Jack looked back at the screen. "If anything changes outside this building, call us."

"Understood," Lena said.

The laboratory remained quiet beyond the muted warning tones at their consoles. Somewhere outside, nearly five hundred people slept through an emergency they did not know existed.

Sarah opened the emitter controls. "Can we interrupt the boundary?"

Jack searched through the manual overrides. "I'm trying."

Sarah pulled up the emitter load. "Could we overload the emitters?"

Jack studied the numbers. "Maybe."

Sarah glanced at him. "Maybe good, or maybe everyone-inside-the-field-dies bad?"

"The second one."

Sarah closed the overload control. "Then let's not."

Jack opened another diagnostic window and searched for a route into the controls. "Come on. Give me something."

The display changed.

"Boundary acquired." Jack's hands stopped. "No."

A second line appeared.

"Scale factor calculating." Sarah pointed to it. "Jack."

Jack kept typing. "I see it."

Sarah studied the command sequence. "Someone isn't just activating TOMBS."

Jack opened another control route. "No."

Sarah looked at him. "They're running it."
