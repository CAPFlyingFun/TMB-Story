## What the chapter does

Three scenes. **The skiff and the work:** Gus Pelletier in person for the first time,
eight in the morning at the station dock, a life jacket before the lines come off,
twenty minutes around the point. High water makes the bar uncrossable in the morning
and makes the landing short and the roost countable, which is why the morning is
right. They close out everything Tuesday's surge left behind: the download off the
unit, the instrument clock read before and after the swap, the spare battery Nora has
carried for four days, the count, the transect reset a stake short, the note to Ines,
the six jars finally dated and still empty. Caleb finds a dew-strung web across the
trail and will not walk through it, so five people go around. **The summit:** the box
Caleb walked past on Tuesday, seen properly. Gus: "That's from the old program.
Before most of us." The seal even and soft, the hasp rivets not weeping, the seam
clean of moss, the dial legible. The stamped tag: `D.M.`, and a year from before the
twins were born. Gus's hand on the lock, "Mercer. Your name's Bennett, though.",
Nora's "Our mother's.", and "Huh." Nora reads the year twice and says nothing. Caleb
sees a bare kneeling-shaped patch in the windrow and forgets it. **The run home:**
the sky thickens, the swell comes around the point, Ines calls it. Nora copies the
file to the lab machine, which is what their mother asked for. Then the coast road,
the trailer sway, the crash, and the dark.

## What the review pass changed, and why

Five specialists reviewed the finished draft. The heaviest findings:

- **The crash was physically impossible.** The road had the water on the right, Marco
  steered right, and the guardrail hit the left side. A right-hand departure cannot
  strike the left. The cut bank is now on the right, the guardrail takes the right
  side, and the ditch at the foot of the bank and the tree follow in order.
- **Caleb could not have unbuckled in an inverted van** without dropping onto the
  roof. The van now goes over onto its side, and he comes up on his knees on what had
  been the side of it.
- **The instrument clock was read only after the battery swap,** which is useless,
  because the record was written on the pre-swap clock. Nora now reads it before and
  again after, which is also more like her.
- **The stern anchor argued against itself** ("She'll dry out where she sits" is the
  thing the anchor prevents). It is now the conditional Gus means.
- **Ines and Marco vanished for the whole summit,** about five hundred words covering
  the chapter's payload, while the frontmatter claimed they heard the name. Each now
  has one placed line at the box, and Marco repeating "Mercer" with no use for it is
  better than either of them being absent.
- **The vision ran 299 words against a 180 ceiling and litigated its own ending.**
  Four denials of the certainty have become one: "Nothing in him had the right to be
  that certain, and he was." The drumming gained a repeating beat, which Chapter 5
  needs so Nora's half can match its rhythm.
- **One clause had the narrator announce the crash** before it happened ("which was
  the last thing that happened before everything else"). Cut.
- **A point-of-view slip:** Caleb's back was turned when Nora read the year, and
  reading is silent. He turns around and watches her do it.
- **Caleb's age sat in the same breath as Nora's doubled year read,** which handed
  the listener a subtraction against Rachel's "Eighteen years ago". The whole
  paragraph is gone. So is a second contact with "eighteen years".
- **Six consecutive negatives described the seals.** A listener cannot picture an
  absence, so half are now positives, and two observations that were Nora's eye in
  Caleb's head were cut.
- **The world specialist's best addition:** the towed boat still has its outboard
  hanging off the transom. Weight aft means a light tongue, which is the actual cause
  of trailer sway. Caleb notices it because he has spent all morning around a skiff.
  It makes the crash nobody's fault without a single character saying so.

Also corrected: "he had never once seen him" now has a reason (the dock is a quarter
mile from the lab); the stake is identified as the one from Caleb's bundle at the
saddle; the weather sequence starts at the dock instead of compressing six hours into
one; the roost is counted through binoculars from a distance; the web on the way down
is caught by the trodden salal of their own detour rather than by seeing an invisible
web; and the ants' stump no longer says "quiet" and "busy" in the same breath.

## Deviations from the card

- **"The tide makes bar access wrong" is only true of the morning.** Saturday's low
  falls about 13:50, so the bar is walkable in the afternoon. The page gives the
  honest reasons for the boat: high water at eight, the roost countable, and Ines
  wanting to be off before the wind. A listener doing the arithmetic will find the
  prose already there.
- **The tag's year is not spoken.** It is "a year from before the twins were born".
  Naming it would fix the series calendar and put the subtraction in the listener's
  hands a chapter early. Flagged as a decision still open.
- **The card's injuries are not all on the page.** Caleb's hand and Nora not moving
  are. Nora's concussion and cracked rib, Marco's bruising and Ines being first on
  scene belong to Chapter 4, per the outline, and the frontmatter no longer claims
  them.
